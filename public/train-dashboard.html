💡 What is happening here? (click to collapse)

🧬 What's a "generation"?

The AI starts with a population of 100 random "brains" (small neural networks). Each brain plays a 60-second game. The worst 5 are discarded, the best 5 are kept, and the other 90 are bred (mix two parents, add small random mutations) to form the next generation. Repeat — the brains get smarter over time.

🏆 What's "fitness"?

A score that rewards good play:

  • +20 / +50 / +100 for destroying large / medium / small asteroids
  • ×10 per second of survival time
  • +100 per power-up picked up

Higher = smarter. The Best line is the top brain; the Average line is the whole population.

📈 How to read the chart

Cyan = best brain (champion of that generation).
Blue = average brain (whole population).
Up-and-to-the-right = learning. Flat line = stuck. Big drop = bad mutations this round.

🚀 How to use the trained brain

1. Wait for a good Best Ever score (or click Stop when you want to keep it).
2. Click Deploy to Game — saves the best brain to public/trained-genome.json.
3. Open localhost:5173 — the game will auto-load the brain.

📚 Training insights — what's actually happening under the hood

👁️ The 13 brain inputs (what the AI "sees")

Each frame, the env builds a 13-number feature vector and feeds it to the neural net:

  1. Speed — how fast it's moving (0 = stopped, 1 = max).
  2. Yaw sin, cos — which way it's facing (a 2D unit vector, so yaw wraps cleanly).
  3. Nearest asteroid dx, dz, dist, radius — where the closest threat is and how big.
  4. Nearest power-up dx, dz, dist — where the pickup is (or "very far" if none).
  5. Laser active — 0 or 1, whether the piercing beam is charged.
  6. Velocity vx, vz — which direction it's flying. This is the key new input (v0.7.0): without it, the brain can't tell "flying right" from "spinning right" — and ends up choosing "spin and shoot" as a local minimum.

All distances are normalized by NORM_DIST = 500 world units. Speeds by MAX_SPEED = 200. This keeps the inputs in a roughly [-1, +1] range so the network doesn't have to learn per-input scaling.

🎯 The 3 brain outputs (what the AI "decides")

The net returns 3 tanh-bounded numbers in [-1, +1]. The trainer discretizes them:

  • Yaw: > 0.33 → turn right, < -0.33 → turn left, else → straight. (The thresholds match the hand-coded AI.)
  • Thrust: > 0 → accelerate, else → coast.
  • Fire: > 0 → pull the trigger (bullet or laser, whichever is active).

This is a very small action space — much smaller than a real human pilot's. A bigger action space (continuous yaw rate, brake, afterburner) would let the brain learn smoother maneuvers, but the trade-off is that bigger actions need more brains + more generations to explore.

🏆 The fitness formula (what the AI is "graded on")

After each episode:

fitness = score + survival × 10 + powerups × 100 + distance × 0.5

The four terms, ranked by typical magnitude:

  • Score — +20 (large) / +50 (medium) / +100 (small) per asteroid destroyed.
  • Survival — +10 per second alive. 60 s episode → +600 if it survives the full time.
  • Power-ups — +100 per pickup. Rare but high-value.
  • Distance (v0.7.0) — +0.5 per world unit traveled. This is the new term: it punishes "just spin in place" and rewards "actually go places." A brain that flies 200 u/s for 30 s nets +3000 from movement alone.

Why the brain was just spinning and shooting: the old formula had no distance term, so the brain had no reason to leave its "spin + fire" local minimum. The new distance term makes that strategy score worse than "fly somewhere." You should see power-up collection emerge within ~30 generations of retraining.

🧬 The evolution loop (how the brain gets smarter)

Every generation, the trainer:

  1. Evaluates every genome by running one (or more) episodes.
  2. Sorts by fitness. Keeps the top 5 (elitism).
  3. Tournament-selects two parents per child, blends their weights 50/50, mutates each weight by a small Gaussian.
  4. Forms the next population of 100, repeats.

Per-episode seed (v0.7.0): the field layout now varies per episode (was fixed). This forces the brain to learn general behavior — without it, the brain can memorize "go to position X" instead of learning "fly toward the nearest asteroid."

Mutation rate is 0.1 (per weight, per child). If you're stuck at a fitness plateau, the mutation rate is the first knob to try: bump it to 0.2 for more exploration, drop to 0.05 for fine-tuning once you're near the goal.

⚙️ Hyperparameter cheat sheet (the dashboard knobs)

KnobDefaultRangeEffect
Population Size10010–500More brains = better exploration, slower per generation.
Hidden Neurons124–64Brain capacity. 12 is good for asteroids; 24+ for trickier tasks.

For power-up collection to emerge reliably, the recommended settings are: Population = 200, Hidden = 24, Duration = 60 s. Train for 100+ generations.

📖 Want to learn more?

  • Sutton & Barto, "Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction" — the canonical free textbook. Chapter 4 (Reward Functions) is the most directly relevant to why the brain behaves the way it does.
  • Kenneth Stanley's NEAT — the original neuroevolution algorithm (we use a simpler GA, not NEAT, but the principles are the same).
  • OpenAI's "Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to Reinforcement Learning" — shows why ES scales better than RL for some problems.
